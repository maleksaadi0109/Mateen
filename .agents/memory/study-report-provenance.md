---
name: Study report provenance
description: Boundaries for reporting practice strengths and daily streaks from saved data.
---

Keep reading progress, automatic practice estimates, and formally passed assessments separate. A partial recitation must not become a mastered hadith just because its few attempted words matched.

**Why:** The user asked for a study-library experience with a contextual assistant and an overview of strong passages and streaks. Existing saved practice snapshots are consented evidence, not certified grades or a complete study-activity ledger.

**How to apply:** Require whole-hadith coverage before describing practice as strong. Report-save summaries must remain explicitly labelled as browser-local save dates, never full study history. Actual continuity must use independently observed engagement, not backfilled report timestamps or sparse saved positions; importing or deleting reports must never rewrite historical study days.

Keep engagement separate from correctness: an attempted recitation can count as study even when words do not match. Do not use inferred omissions as spoken-word evidence.

**Why:** Continuity encourages actual practice, whereas the existing recognizer can infer omissions or misrecognize speech. Neither is evidence of exam mastery, and correct-only counting would discourage learners who most need practice.

Use a fixed per-account calendar for continuity across devices and travel; any future timezone-change feature needs an explicit history-preservation policy.

**Why:** Silently following each browser's timezone could change day boundaries and apparent continuity without new study.
